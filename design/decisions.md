# Decisions — Claim Bite

Append-only, newest first. `date · decision · why`. Standing non-goals live here too.

- 2026-09-03 · A missed swing pays 0 ore; a hit stays at 3 · owner call, reversing the agent call of 2026-09-02 that had a miss paying 1. Missing now costs the whole swing, which makes the timing bar the yield dial it was always meant to be

- 2026-09-03 · The Market is a 5m proximity zone around a `Market` entity: a grid of five items (Pick, Shovel, Wheelbarrow, House, M.U.L.E.), select one and a Buy button appears; the button is disabled and names the shortfall when coins are short · owner call. Closes the third verb of §3, "Spend"

- 2026-09-03 · M.U.L.E. is taken to be the idle rig, so it carries the GDD's fixed 100-coin price · agent call, pending owner review. Shovel (25), Wheelbarrow (60) and House (500) are pure placeholders — only the pick's 10 and the rig's 100 come from the GDD

- 2026-09-02 · A sale is priced ore by ore down a descending ladder, not at one flat rate: the first unit of a batch fetches today's price and each following unit a notch less · agent call, pending owner review: at a flat rate, dumping the whole bag is always optimal and the amount selector would be decoration. Measured at the placeholder numbers — 100 ore dumped at once pays 150 coins, the same 100 sold in four batches 15s apart pays 188

- 2026-09-02 · The ore price moves linearly in both directions with a hard floor · owner call: two dials that can be balanced by hand, per the 2026-08-31 call. Placeholders in `src/economy/constants.ts` — base 2.00 coins/ore, floor 0.50, −0.01 per ore sold, +0.02/s recovery

- 2026-09-02 · Selling takes an amount, not a single "sell all" button: ±1 / ±10 steppers plus Half and All shortcuts · owner call, chosen over the one-button option. Adds a second decision (how much) on top of the GDD's original one (when)

- 2026-09-02 · The bank is a 5m proximity zone around the `Bank` entity, the same pattern as the dig's 3m zone · owner call. Both use a per-frame distance check rather than `pointerEventsSystem.onProximityEnter`: proximity events are new in SDK 7.27 and an older Explorer never emits them, which is exactly what silently broke the dig's first implementation

- 2026-09-02 · A missed swing still pays ore (placeholder: hit 3, miss 1), never zero · agent call, pending owner review: a zero-payout miss turns the timing bar into a punishment in a game §8 explicitly says is not reflex-based — the gap between hit and miss carries §3's "a well-timed hit pays more" on its own. Both numbers are TBD and live in `src/economy/constants.ts`

- 2026-09-02 · Scene geometry (town, quarry, buildings) is authored in the Creator Hub inspector, not in code; code finds those entities by name and attaches behaviour · owner call: the art is the owner's own job and the inspector is where it happens. Accepted tradeoff: `main.crdt` is binary, so scene edits can't be reviewed in a diff and only one of us touches it at a time

- 2026-09-02 · Game state (ore, coins, and later the bank price, the rig, the house) lives in plain-data modules under `src/state/`, with no ECS, React or rendering imports · owner call: client-only is fast enough to build and tune the week-1 loop, and keeping state render-free means the same module moves to the authoritative server in week 2 (§9) without rewriting anything that draws

- 2026-09-02 · The pick also expires after one real-world week regardless of use, on top of its existing per-swing wear · owner call: per-swing wear stays as the sell-timing pressure (Pillar 2); the weekly backstop just stops a rarely-used pick from lasting forever. Kept cheap on purpose — a starting pick is a flat 10 coins, so this is a light nudge, not a punishment

- 2026-09-02 · Return hook 2 replaced: "persistent building progression" (the next tier of your claim/rig/house, permanent and visible to others) instead of the bank's async trace · owner call: the trace made the world feel alive, but never gave a real reason to come BACK — the promise of building something real and lasting does. The trace itself stays in the design (§3 repetition-10, §5 disappearance test), it just stopped pulling double duty as a return hook
- 2026-09-01 · **Team reality:** solo, full-time, one generalist doing programming, 3D modeling, texturing and UI · owner call — load-bearing for §9: every week's plan competes code time against art time on the same person, so scope has to assume that, not a team
- 2026-08-31 · **Standing north star:** success is a small core of loyal players (owner's number: ~10) who play daily and visibly build their own empire — not raw casual headcount. Casuals are pulled in by seeing that core exist, not targeted directly · owner call — shapes what "the ideal group" and "ambitious" mean everywhere downstream (§5 population numbers, §8 primary player, any growth framing)
- 2026-08-31 · The town and the mine are two separate places: the mine is claims and quarries at the foot of the mountains outside town; the town (one street, a couple of blocks) is where the bank and player-owned houses stand · owner call: a mining rig sitting inside a mansion didn't read right — splitting them also gives the world two legible zones instead of one
- 2026-08-31 · Wealth is shown as a house in town, upgradable toward a mansion, instead of a numeric leaderboard · owner call: the goal is a player feeling the world is visibly theirs, not a row in a table — replaces the earlier "gilded gear" placeholder for the week-3+ status marker
- 2026-08-31 · The "boom town" bonus: mining in the public dig near other players raises everyone's yield there (roughly +10% per other miner, capped near +20%) · owner-picked from a 4-option menu — direct, immediate, and cheap (a headcount check + a multiplier), unlike the shared price's indirect, day-later effect
- 2026-08-31 · Direct player-vs-player sabotage (theft, griefing, organized moves against the richest player) stays cut for the MVP · owner call, after noting the shared dynamic price already punishes a big spender's own market for free — revisit only if that isn't enough friction on its own
- 2026-08-31 · The idle rig's ore accumulates up to a cap, then stops — claiming it requires an explicit action, it does not auto-collect · owner call: gives a real, non-punishing reason to come back ("don't leave production on the table") without a hard deadline
- 2026-08-31 · Land rent is deducted automatically from the coin balance — no manual payment action, ever · owner call: refines the earlier "recurring cost" decision — a daily obligation to physically show up and pay was too demanding for a casual game; if the balance can't cover it, the idle machine still just pauses (the earlier no-debt, no-eviction call stands)
- 2026-08-31 · The bank keeps a short async log of who sold and when, visible even after the price itself has recovered · H1-02 (`failed`) showed the shared price alone can't carry "other players are the variety" at Decentraland's real 0-3 CCU baseline — most scenes are empty most of the time. The log is the fix, and it now carries part of Pillar 1
- 2026-08-31 · World is a half-abandoned gold-rush mining town, one street, wooden — every interactable carries a magenta emissive outline, a color the rest of the palette never uses · owner call on the world; agent call on magenta over green — nothing in a wood/dirt/rock town is naturally magenta, so it reads with zero ambiguity, where green risked blending with any dead grass or cactus dressing
- 2026-08-31 · Land rent stays simple: a recurring cost, and if unpaid the idle machine just pauses until renewed — no debt, no eviction, no player-to-player land trade in the MVP · owner call: keeps the original cut (loans/foreclosure, land trade — both still fase 2+) cut, while still giving the recurring sink the idle path needed
- 2026-08-31 · Land is rented, not bought outright, and renting is what unlocks placing an idle machine · connects to the original two-parallel-investment design (manual vs. idle) — no idle production without land, no reason to want land without idle
- 2026-08-31 · The player does not start already owning a plot. Spawn is a welcome from an NPC (the mayor), who hands over a free pick silently — no dialogue explaining how to mine · keeps first-use clarity intact (the ore vein and the tap bar still teach the verb) while giving the town a face on arrival
- 2026-08-31 · Before renting a plot, a player can mine in a public zone at reduced yield · resolves the bootstrap problem (no coins yet to rent anything) and turns "get your own plot" into the first session's real goal instead of a freebie handed at spawn — supersedes the earlier "parcel at spawn" framing
- 2026-08-31 · §1's optional "familiar comparison" line dropped · agent call: the one grounded comparable (Tap Space) is too obscure to anchor a reader's mental picture, and the promise already stands on its own without it
- 2026-08-31 · Gold-rush / western is a setting decision, not a locked pillar — a western-steampunk skin is open, but the gold-rush frame stays as the player's motivating context · owner call: the mechanics don't require any specific period skin, but the fantasy of striking it rich is why the loop makes sense to a new player
- 2026-08-31 · Tool and equipment purchase prices are fixed (e.g. pick = 10 coins, idle rig = 100 coins) · only the bank's ore buy price stays dynamic — a fixed catalogue is easier to balance by hand than tuning every item against demand
- 2026-08-31 · The bank's ore price is dynamic (falls with player selling, recovers over time), not fixed · it is the one thing that makes two players mining at once feel like the same town instead of two separate saves — and a formula with two dials is simpler to balance than a hand-tuned catalogue would be
- 2026-08-31 · Two currencies: raw ore is mined, coins are legal tender, and only the bank converts one into the other · it turns selling from a formality into a timing decision — ore cannot buy anything, so holding for a better price has a real cost
- 2026-08-31 · Selling is player-timed, never automatic on a full bag · the timing choice is the decision the loop is built on
- 2026-08-31 · Claiming a plot is the first-minutes beat, not a core-loop step · it happens once per player, so it cannot be part of a cycle that repeats
- 2026-08-31 · Two nested loops: a short mine-and-sell cycle, with spending on tools running at a slower rhythm on top · an upgrade cannot be bought every cycle, and folding it in would push the cycle far past a mobile-length session
